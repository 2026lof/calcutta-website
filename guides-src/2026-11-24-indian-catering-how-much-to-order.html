You've got a birthday, an office lunch or the whole family coming over, and you'd rather not cook. Indian food is made for this: curries hold their heat, rice and naan stretch a long way, and there's something for vegetarians, vegans and meat lovers on the same table. The tricky part is knowing how much to order. Here's the simple plan we give people who call us in Ardsley.

Start with a simple formula

For a full meal, plan on about one main-dish serving per guest, spread across a few different curries, plus rice and naan for everyone. Mixing several dishes beats ordering a lot of one: people like to take a little of everything.

  • Mains: one chicken or meat dish for every 8 to 10 guests, and at least one vegetarian dish for every 10.
  • Rice: enough for everyone. A biryani counts as rice and a main at the same time.
  • Naan: about one piece per guest, a little more for a hungry crowd.
  • Starters: two or three pieces per guest, like samosas or kebabs.
  • Extras: raita (cool yogurt) and salad balance out the spice.

Dishes almost everyone likes

If your guests are new to Indian food, these are safe bets: Butter Chicken and Chicken Tikka Masala (creamy, mild tomato sauces), Chicken Biryani (fragrant rice with chicken and whole spices), Chana Masala (chickpeas, and vegan) and Dal Makhani (slow-cooked black lentils). Add Chicken Reshmi Kebab or Chicken Tikka from the tandoor for something off the grill.

Plan for every diet

Indian food makes it easy to feed vegetarians and vegans without a separate meal. Chana Masala, Aloo Gobi, Dal Tadka, Rajma and Baingan Bhartha are all on our vegan menu, and most curries are served with rice rather than bread, which helps guests avoiding gluten. Just tell us about allergies when you order.

Spice: go mild for a crowd

We cook most dishes mild, medium or hot. For a mixed group, order mild and add one hotter dish, like a vindaloo, for the guests who want the heat. Nobody gets left out.
