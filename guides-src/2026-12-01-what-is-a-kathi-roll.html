Walk down Park Street in Calcutta (now Kolkata) at night and you'll smell them before you see them: paratha sizzling on a hot griddle, kebabs coming off the grill, a cook wrapping it all up in paper. That's the kathi roll, and it's the reason our restaurant has "roll" in its name.

What's in a kathi roll

  • The wrap: a paratha, a flaky, layered flatbread cooked on a griddle until crisp at the edges and soft inside.
  • The egg: the Calcutta way, an egg is fried right onto the paratha before the filling goes in. That golden layer is what makes a real Calcutta roll.
  • The filling: grilled kebabs (chicken, lamb or paneer) or a spiced filling.
  • The crunch and the kick: raw onion, green chilies, a squeeze of lime and chutney.

Then it's rolled tight and wrapped in paper, so you can eat it with one hand on the way to wherever you're going.

Where it comes from

The kathi roll is usually credited to Nizam's, a restaurant in Calcutta's New Market, early in the 20th century. As the story goes, customers wanted their kebabs to go, so the cooks wrapped them in paratha. "Kathi" refers to the bamboo skewers the kebabs were cooked on. A century later, rolls are sold on street corners all over the city, and Calcutta still takes them very seriously.

How to eat one

Peel the paper back as you go and eat it warm. One roll makes a good lunch; two make a dinner. They travel well, so they're our most popular pickup order for a quick lunch in Ardsley, a commute home through Dobbs Ferry or a game night in Hastings.
