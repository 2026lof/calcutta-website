An office holiday party has to please everyone: the adventurous eater, the cautious one, the vegan in accounting and the boss who "doesn't do spicy." Indian food handles all of them on one table, and it's a nice change from another round of sandwiches. Here's a menu we'd put together for a team in Westchester.

A menu that works for everyone

Starters

  • Vegetable Samosas: crisp pastry filled with spiced potatoes and peas. Everyone's favorite.
  • Chicken Reshmi Kebab: creamy, mild and grilled in the tandoor.
  • Pakora: vegetable fritters that disappear fast.

Mains

  • Butter Chicken and Chicken Tikka Masala: the two dishes nobody argues about.
  • Lamb Korma: rich and mild, for the meat lovers.
  • Palak Paneer: spinach with Indian cheese, for vegetarians.
  • Chana Masala and Aloo Gobi: both vegan, and both hearty enough to be a main.
  • Dal Makhani: slow-cooked black lentils, the comfort food of the table.

Rice, bread and sides

  • Chicken Biryani and Vegetable Biryani
  • Plain and Garlic Naan
  • Raita and Fresh Calcutta Salad

Four tips for a smooth party

  1. Order mild. Most dishes can be made mild, medium or hot. Make the mains mild and add one hotter dish for the spice fans.
  2. Label everything. Ask us which dishes are vegetarian, vegan and spicy, and put a small card by each tray so nobody has to guess.
  3. Count the vegetarians. In most offices it's more people than you think. Two or three meat-free mains is about right.
  4. Book early. We need at least two days' notice for catering, and December fills up, so order as early as you can.
